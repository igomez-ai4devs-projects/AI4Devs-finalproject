## Suggested drill order

This is an **architectural build sequence**, not a value ranking: with nothing built, dependency depth dominates. It is a recommendation; the Product Owner and the team pick.

1. **`C10` — Identity & Access Management.** The phase-0 anchor. It carries the entire workspace foundation (4 applications, 4 shared libraries, the design system, the base schema and migration chain) plus authentication and RBAC. `FR-IAM-01` ("The system MUST authenticate every user before granting access to any function") is a precondition of every other epic: Sport ITSM has no anonymous surface at all. Prerequisite of **18** epics. PRD §14.2 places **`FR-IAM-01/02/03/05`** in Phase 0 — but as of the 2026-09-24 PRD revision the epic spans four phases and is **no longer phase-atomic**: `FR-IAM-06`/`FR-IAM-08`/`FR-IAM-09` are Phase 1, `FR-IAM-07` is Phase 2 and `FR-IAM-04` is Phase 3. Drill position 1 is unchanged; what changes is that a `C10` drill must slice by phase rather than deliver all 9 requirements as one increment. See finding **F6**. **Before resuming this epic, read finding F14**: `FR-IAM-09` is half-delivered through the backlog — decided in the PRD, three partial stories, a fourth missing, no tickets — and it reaches into four other capabilities.
2. **`C18` — Audit Trail & Activity History.** `ARCHITECTURE.md` §4.1 places the `audit` context in **phase 0**, alongside `identity-access`. Audit is an append-only consumer of domain events; it must exist before any context begins emitting, otherwise every later epic retrofits event publication. `FR-IAM-05` requires role assignment to be "fully audited", so `C10` and `C18` are genuinely co-phase-0 (see finding **F5**).
3. **`C12` — Workflow & Automation Engine.** Realized as the `StateModel` / transition-rule primitive in `libs/shared/domain` plus per-context configuration (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §4.1 — a central workflow-engine context is explicitly rejected). Every lifecycle in the product instantiates it: `FR-INC-06`, `FR-SRQ-05`, `FR-CHG-04`, `FR-REL-03`, `FR-PRB-03`. Building it after the first lifecycle means rewriting that lifecycle.
4. **`C14` — Assignment & Queue Management.** Resolver Groups live in the `identity-access` context (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §4.1: `identity-access` = C10 **and** C14), so it creates no new libraries and follows `C10` naturally. Incident assignment (`FR-INC-12`), automatic routing (`FR-WFL-03`) and escalation (`FR-INC-13`, `FR-SLA-07`) all target groups that must already exist. PRD §14.2 places Resolver Groups in Phase 0.
5. **`C7` — SLA Management & Escalation.** The conscious **upstream supplier** to both ticket contexts (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §4.3, Customer-Supplier). `FR-SLA-02` requires exactly one applicable SLA policy to be attached to each ticket **at creation** — the policy registry, the support schedule and the timer semantics must exist before the first Incident can be logged correctly. Building `C1` first forces a stub port and a rework of `FR-INC-08` and `FR-SLA-04`.

### `C10` · Identity & Access Management (PRD §7.10)

- **Requirements (9):** `FR-IAM-01` 🔴 M · `FR-IAM-02` 🔴 M · `FR-IAM-03` 🔴 M · `FR-IAM-04` 🔴 S · `FR-IAM-05` 🔴 M · `FR-IAM-06` 🔴 S · `FR-IAM-07` 🔴 C · `FR-IAM-08` 🔴 M · `FR-IAM-09` 🔴 M
- **Phase spread (PRD §14, revised 2026-09-24):** Phase 0 — `FR-IAM-01/02/03/05` · Phase 1 — `FR-IAM-06`, `FR-IAM-08`, `FR-IAM-09` · Phase 2 — `FR-IAM-07` · Phase 3 — `FR-IAM-04`
- **What actually remains:** Everything, plus the whole workspace. This epic stands up the Nx monorepo, the module-boundary enforcement, the four applications (`api`, `api-e2e`, `web`, `web-e2e`), the four shared libraries (`contracts`, `domain`, `ui`, `util`), the PostgreSQL base schema and the migration chain, and only then its own five `identity-access` libraries (`domain`, `application`, `infrastructure`, `feature`, `data-access` — no `ui` of its own per `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5.1). Functionally: Passport-JWT authentication with no anonymous surface anywhere (`FR-IAM-01`), persona-aligned RBAC with least privilege (`FR-IAM-02`), requester-scoped record visibility with the competition-scoped exception for Organizer and League Administrator (`FR-IAM-03`), an `IdentityProviderPort` anti-corruption layer for SCMS SSO (`FR-IAM-04`, assumption A2, dependency D1), audited role assignment (`FR-IAM-05`), session inactivity termination and step-up re-authentication at the moment of a privileged action (`FR-IAM-06`), user-initiated sign-out so a shared device can be handed over deliberately and every audit entry stays attributable (`FR-IAM-08`), and denied-authorization logging for privileged operations (`FR-IAM-07`). **Constraint on how the session requirements may be built:** PRD **§14.8** fixes the assurance level at **device-bounded termination** — a bounded session lifetime plus device-side termination, explicitly **not** validation of each request against a stored, per-request-updated record of live sessions. That is a product decision with named reversal triggers, not an implementation preference, and no story or ticket under this epic may specify the stronger form. **`FR-IAM-09` is the widest-reaching requirement in this epic and the one most likely to be under-sized:** lawful erasure must render a person non-identifying across users, tickets, comments, notifications, exports, reports and audit history — so it touches nearly every context in the product, even though it is owned here. It also carries the PRD's **only authorized exception to audit immutability** (`FR-AUD-03`), narrowly scoped to neutralizing personal data inside an otherwise preserved entry, plus a precedence rule over retention (`FR-AUD-06`) and an irreversibility obligation. Drill it late within the epic, after the audit discipline exists, and do not let it be reduced to "delete the user row". **Three further behaviors were added to it at `b129e03`**, after its first stories were written, and they are the parts most likely to be missed:
  1. **Intake and legitimacy.** A request arrives either as a Service Request from the seventh catalog offering (`FR-SRQ-09`, owned by `C2`/`C8`) or **agent-logged on the person's behalf** (`FR-OMN-01`) — the second path is mandatory, because the product has no anonymous surface (`FR-IAM-01`) and the data subject may never have been a user at all. **Sport ITSM does not judge lawfulness**: that decision is made outside the product (assumption `A10`) and enters as the **approval that gates execution** (`FR-SRQ-04`, `FR-APR-03`, immutable under `FR-APR-07`) — so `C15` is on the critical path of an erasure.
  2. **Authentication ends with the identity.** The account is permanently deactivated, may never be reactivated (explicitly outside `FR-SRQ-09`'s reactivation offering), its roles are revoked and audited, and its former identifiers may never be reused. The account record **survives as a non-identifying shell** because audit entries reference the actor. Consistently with §14.8 there is **no central withdrawal** of a session already in progress: the guarantee is that no *new* session can be established.
  3. **Open work never defers erasure.** It proceeds with open tickets, pending approvals and active fulfillment tasks — deferring would contradict the rule that retention may not delay a request. The work survives, the person does not; and each contact-dependent path is closed explicitly: no notifications to an erased person (`C16`), records awaiting their information/confirmation/CSAT closed with a reason rather than ageing against SLA, approval tasks withdrawn and re-resolved (`C15`), delivery-only fulfillment tasks cancelled (`C2`).
- **Depends on:** `C18` (declared — `FR-IAM-05` requires role assignment to be "fully audited"; mutual, see **F5**)
- **Size:** **XL** — unchanged, but now the band is doing real work rather than being carried by the foundation alone. 9 requirements, and the +2 from `FR-IAM-08`/`FR-IAM-09` does not move the band: the driver is the foundation, not the requirement count — 4 applications + 9 libraries created from nothing, the tag scheme, the boundary matrix, the design system and the migration chain.
